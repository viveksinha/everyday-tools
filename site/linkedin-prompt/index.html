      How each check works · and its limits
      
    
        
      
          
    • Characters count each emoji and accented letter as one. LinkedIn's post limit is 3,000.
      • 
          
    • Sentences split at . ! ? and line breaks. Variation is the standard deviation of words per sentence; under 4 words with 4+ sentences reads as flat.
      • 
          
    • Long paragraphs estimate lines at about 80 characters each, so treat them as a hint.
      • 
          
    • Links in the body are flagged because many writers report lower reach for them; LinkedIn doesn't confirm this.
      • 
          
    • Phrase list is a style choice, not a rule. Cut what reads like filler to you.

    About
    
    
    
      
    
        
    How to use it
    
        
      
          
    • Build a prompt from your facts, copy it, paste it into the assistant you use.
      • 
          
    • Check the draft it gives back, fix what's flagged, read it aloud once.
      • 
          
    • Or skip the assistant: take a template and write it yourself.
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Privacy
    
        
      
          
    • Runs in your browser. This page has no AI and makes no API calls.
      • 
          
    • Nothing is stored or sent. No cookies, accounts or tracking.
      • 
          
    • Build-tab text sits in the page link, so don't share the link if the facts are private.
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Why these rules
    
        
      
          
    • Invented details are the fastest way to lose trust, so the prompt bans them.
      • 
          
    • Stock phrases and upbeat spin make posts read as machine-written and skimmable.
      • 
          
    • The first line is often all people see, so it has to stand alone.
      • 
        
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Sources (checked Nov 2026)
    
        
      
          
    • 3,000-character post limit: given by both AuthoredUp and ContentIn as LinkedIn's stated cap.
      • 
          
    • "See more" at about 210 characters on desktop, 140 on phones: AuthoredUp's figures. ContentIn says the cut-off isn't officially documented and varies by device and formatting.
      • 
          
    • Preview in LinkedIn's own composer before posting; the real cut-off can differ from these figures.
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Limits
    
        
      
          
    • The checks are heuristics. A clean score doesn't make a good post.
      • 
          
    • LinkedIn changes its layout; the cut-off can move without notice.
      • 
          
    • Assistants don't always follow rules, so check the output every time.
