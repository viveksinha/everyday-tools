Doses use the lab number where there is one
Why the lab number matters
  • Most products hold more than the label says, up to 40% more here.
  • Dose by the lab number. A "220 mg" tablet can really be ~283 mg.
The 350 mg ceiling
  • 350 mg a day is the adult upper limit for magnesium from supplements. Food magnesium doesn't count.
  • Going over mainly risks diarrhoea and an upset stomach.
Not medical advice
  • Check with a doctor first if you have kidney problems or take other medicines.

How to read this

About the ratings
  • A rating covers one tested batch, not whatever stock is on the shelf now.
  • Ratings last 12 months from the date they go live. This page marks them expired once that date passes.
  • "Initiated by" says who paid for or asked for the test: the brand, Unbox Health, or a community vote.
About the numbers
  • Magnesium is per capsule or tablet as tested; the iThrive powder is per serving.
  • 0 ppm means below the detection limit, so traces may still be there.
  • "vs label" above 0% means the lab found more magnesium than the label states.

Glossary

Elemental magnesiumThe actual magnesium in a compound. Magnesium bisglycinate is only about 14% magnesium by weight, so labels should state elemental mg.
Glycinate / bisglycinateMagnesium bound to glycine. Some "glycinate" products are buffered with cheaper magnesium oxide.
ChelatedA mineral bound to an amino acid. Usually gentler on the stomach than magnesium oxide.
ppmParts per million. 1 ppm is 1 microgram per gram of product.
Non-toxicity scoreUnbox Health's 0–10 score for heavy-metal safety. 10 is best.
Label accuracy scoreUnbox Health's 0–10 score for how closely the lab result matches the label. 10 is best.
NABLIndia's National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories.
FSSAIFood Safety and Standards Authority of India. It sets the legal heavy-metal limits for supplements.
