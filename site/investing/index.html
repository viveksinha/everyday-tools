Reading the result
  • Gains grow faster later because returns compound on earlier returns.
  • Today's ₹ divides by inflation, so you can compare with prices now.
  • A step-up raises the SIP once a year, often in line with pay rises.
About the return
  • Real returns vary year to year; equity funds can fall 30% or more in a bad year.
  • A fixed rate shows the average path, not the ups and downs.
  • Value is before tax on redemption and after the fund's expense ratio.
Tax when you sell
  • Equity funds: 12.5% on long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year, 20% within 12 months.
  • Each SIP instalment has its own 12-month clock.
  • Debt funds bought since April 2023 are taxed at your slab.

How it's worked out
  • Future goal = today's amount × (1 + inflation)years.
  • Monthly SIP = future goal ÷ what ₹1 a month (with the same step-up) grows to.
  • Lumpsum = future goal ÷ (1 + return ÷ 12)months.
Making it safer
  • Use a lower return than you hope for; shortfalls hurt more than surpluses.
  • Education and health costs often rise faster than general inflation.
  • Move to safer funds in the last few years so a crash doesn't hit the goal.

After-tax yearly return

Fixed deposit
  • Interest is taxed every year at your slab, even in a cumulative FD that pays out at maturity.
  • TDS of 10% applies when a bank's interest to you passes ₹50,000 a year (₹1 lakh if 60+); it counts toward your tax.
  • Here the yearly tax comes out of the deposit, interest compounds quarterly.
Debt fund
  • Units bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your slab, however long you hold them.
  • No indexation, but tax is due only when you redeem, so the money compounds untaxed until then.
  • Applies to funds with more than 65% in debt and money-market instruments.
Arbitrage fund
  • Taxed as an equity fund because it holds at least 65% in hedged equity.
  • 20% within 12 months; 12.5% after that on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year.
  • Returns track short-term rates and may be a little lower after Budget 2026 raised STT on futures.
Assumptions · what this comparison leaves out
  • 4% cess is added to every tax; surcharge for incomes above ₹50 lakh is ignored.
  • The ₹1.25 lakh exemption is assumed unused by other equity gains, and used once, in the year you sell.
  • "Years" of 1 or more counts as long-term for the arbitrage fund (more than 12 months held).
  • The 87A rebate and the basic exemption limit are ignored; pick 0% if your income is under the taxable limit.
  • Fund returns are after expenses and aren't guaranteed; FD rates are fixed for the term.

About this calculator

SIP convention
  • Monthly compounding at the annual return ÷ 12.
  • Each instalment is paid at the start of the month, so it earns that month's return.
  • Check: ₹10,000 a month at 12% for 10 years = ₹10,000 × ((1.01120 − 1) ÷ 0.01) × 1.01 = ₹23,23,391.
Other choices
  • Step-up raises the SIP at the start of each new year.
  • Lumpsum is invested on day one at the same return.
  • Funds in the FD tab grow at the yearly rate you enter (annual compounding).
Estimate, not advice
  • These are estimates, not financial or tax advice.
  • Past returns don't predict future ones; fund values go down as well as up.
  • Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.
