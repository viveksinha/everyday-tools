Whey types explained

Concentrate vs isolate vs hydrolysate vs blends vs plant
The short version
  • All whey comes from milk, the liquid left after making paneer or cheese.
  • More filtering means more protein, less lactose and fat, and a higher price.

Which should you choose?
  • No lactose issues, want best value: unflavoured concentrate (WPC80).
  • Bloating or gas with milk, or strict on carbs and fat: isolate.
  • Want it to taste good with water: a flavoured isolate or a good blend. Check sugar, and that protein is still 70 g+ per 100 g.
  • Dairy-free or vegan: a pea + rice blend or yeast protein. Expect to pay a bit more per gram of protein.
  • Hydrolysate is rarely worth the extra money for everyday use.
Reading a label quickly
  1. Look at protein per 100 g, not per scoop. Scoops run 25–45 g, so per-scoop numbers mislead.
  2. Check ingredient order. "Whey protein isolate" alone is a true isolate. Concentrate, whey powder or milk solids first means a blend.
  3. "No added sugar" isn't no sugar. Milk, fruit and dates add their own; check total sugar or total carbs.
  4. Extras take up weight. Creatine, yeast protein, safed musli and vitamins lower the protein %.
  5. Sodium runs from 23.5 to 774 mg per 100 g in this list. It matters if you watch blood pressure.
  6. Prefer independent testing (Trustified, Labdoor, Informed Choice) and a batch report you can look up.
  7. Compare ₹ per gram of protein: price ÷ (pack grams × protein per 100 g ÷ 100).

Glossary

BCAABranched-chain amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine). Whey is ~22–25% BCAA, so ~5–7 g per scoop.
Amino spikingAdding cheap amino acids to push up the protein number on the label.
LeucineThe amino acid that switches on muscle building. Whey has a lot (~10% of protein); plant proteins have less.
Dry basisProtein % measured with moisture removed. Reads a little higher than "as is".
Cross-flow micro-filtration (CFM)A gentle filtering method used for isolates that keeps proteins intact.
InstantisedCoated with a little lecithin so it mixes without clumps.
Trustified / Labdoor / Informed ChoiceIndependent labs that test supplements for protein content and banned substances. Trustified tests Indian products blind.

How scoring works

same formulas as the source spreadsheet
In one line each
  • Five part scores from 0 to 100, combined with your weights.
  • Missing label values are skipped, not treated as zero, and the other weights are rescaled.
  • Whey competes with whey; plant and yeast compete with each other.
What gets ranked
  • Only confirmed ("Live") prices are ranked. Others still get a score.
  • Plant and yeast aren't in the overall rank because protein quality differs.
Known biases
  • Value favours cheap, lower-protein tubs like Bigmuscles. Try "Quality first" to lean the other way.
  • Lab status is the brand's or seller's claim, not checked against certifier databases.
