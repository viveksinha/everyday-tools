Best for

General patterns; run your own numbers in the calculator
Very high monthly spend
  • Magnus for Burgundy earns 35 points per ₹200 on spend above ₹1.5 lakh a month, about 14% at transfer value.
  • The ₹30,000 fee needs ₹30 lakh a year to be waived, so it suits ₹2.5 lakh+ a month.
  • Transfer caps (2 lakh + 8 lakh points a year) limit how much of that you can move to airlines.
Booking travel through a bank portal
  • Infinia Metal earns 10X on SmartBuy hotels and 5X on flights, with the highest HDFC cap (15,000 extra points a month).
  • HSBC Premier and ICICI offer 12X hotels and 6X flights with 18,000-point caps.
  • Diners Black Metal has the same rates as Infinia but a 10,000-point cap.
Spending abroad
  • HSBC Premier charges 0.99% forex markup; the other four charge 2%.
  • Diners Black Metal offers an optional Global Value Program: 1% back on foreign spend for ₹199 + GST a year (not modelled).
Rent, insurance and bills
  • HSBC Premier still earns on rent, utilities, insurance and education, up to ₹1 lakh a month combined.
  • Magnus for Burgundy earns on the first ₹50,000 of rent a month but nothing on utilities or insurance.
  • HDFC and ICICI pay nothing on rent and cap utilities, groceries and insurance.
Keeping fees low
  • HSBC Premier has no renewal fee while you keep a Premier relationship.
  • Diners Black Metal has the lowest spend waiver, ₹8 lakh a year.
  • Infinia Metal and Emeralde Private Metal waive at ₹10 lakh.
Who can get them
  • Open application: only Diners Black Metal, on income.
  • Banking relationship: Magnus for Burgundy (Axis Burgundy) and HSBC Premier (HSBC Premier).
  • Invite-only: Infinia Metal and Emeralde Private Metal.

How the calculator works

Points
  • Each category earns the card's base rate, minus exclusions, then any monthly category cap is applied.
  • Portal spend earns the portal multiple; only the extra points count against the portal's monthly cap.
  • Magnus for Burgundy adds 23 extra points per ₹200 on spend above ₹1.5 lakh a month, up to your credit limit.
  • One month is multiplied by 12, so it assumes the same spend every month.
Point value
  • Cash is the floor: statement credit or the bank's catalogue rate.
  • Portal is what a point buys on the bank's own flight and hotel portal.
  • Transfer is the airline ratio times your mile value (₹1 by default; premium-cabin awards can be worth more).
Net value
  • Renewal fee plus 18% GST is subtracted unless your yearly eligible spend clears the waiver.
  • Forex markup plus GST on international spend is subtracted too.
  • Effective return is net value divided by total yearly spend.
What it leaves out
  • Welcome and renewal points, unless you tick the box.
  • Lounge access, golf, memberships and insurance have no rupee value here.
  • Rounding: banks award points per full ₹100/150/200 per transaction, so real totals run slightly lower.
  • Diners weekend dining 2X and limited-time portal offers.
Where sources disagree
  • Axis partner ratios change without notice; trackers in Sept 2026 list Accor and Cathay at 5:2.
  • Diners Black Metal caps (grocery, utilities, insurance, per-statement) come from trackers, not HDFC's card page.
  • ICICI point values and iShop rates aren't published by ICICI; trackers give ₹0.25–0.40 cash.
  • HSBC cash value isn't published; ₹0.25 is a placeholder.
Before you apply
  • This is an estimate, not financial advice.
  • Terms change often; check the bank's MITC and reward T&C before you decide.
  • Fee waivers may exclude more categories than modelled here (Axis excludes gift cards from 28 Aug 2026).

Worked example · Magnus for Burgundy at ₹3 lakh a month
  • First ₹1.5 lakh: 12 points per ₹200 = 9,000 points.
  • Next ₹1.5 lakh: 35 points per ₹200 = 26,250 points.
  • Month: 35,250 points; year: 4,23,000 points. At 5:4 and ₹1 a mile that is ₹3,38,400.
  • ₹36 lakh a year clears the ₹30 lakh waiver, so no fee.
