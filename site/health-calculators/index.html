Where the ranges come from
  • 0.83 g/kg is the adult RDA in the ICMR-NIN 2024 dietary guidelines.
  • 1.2–1.6 g/kg for regular exercise sits in the lower half of the 1.2–2.0 range in the 2016 ACSM, AND and DC sports position.
  • 1.6–2.2 g/kg for strength training: a 2018 meta-analysis found gains levelled off around 1.6 (95% CI 1.03–2.20).
  • 1.0–1.2 g/kg for over-65s is the PROT-AGE group's advice.
Spreading it out
  • The ISSN suggests about 0.25 g/kg, or 20–40 g, per meal.
  • Even meals are the simplest way to get there.
Before you add protein
  • ICMR-NIN advises against protein powders for healthy people; food usually covers the need.
  • Kidney disease: high intakes may not be safe; ask a doctor.
  • Pregnancy, illness and children have different needs not covered here.

How much is a lot
  • 400 mg a day is the level the US FDA and EU's EFSA see as safe for most healthy adults.
  • 200 mg at once is EFSA's single-dose figure.
  • Pregnancy: EFSA's figure is 200 mg a day from all sources.
How the curve is drawn
  • Each drink counts in full the moment you have it; real levels peak 15 minutes to 2 hours later.
  • Every half-life, half of what's left is cleared.
  • The day runs 5 am to 5 am, so a 1 am drink counts as late night.
Sleep
  • There's no agreed safe level at bedtime; sensitivity differs a lot.
  • Moving the last cup earlier lowers the bedtime figure most.

Worth checking
  • Overlap: a multivitamin may already contain what a single supplement adds.
