Reading the result
  • Total tax includes surcharge (above ₹50 lakh) and 4% cess.
  • Break-even is the total of old-regime-only deductions (HRA, 80C, 80D, home loan, own NPS) at which both regimes cost the same.
  • Employer NPS is allowed in both, so it doesn't count toward the break-even.
New regime in short
  • Default regime; salaried people can switch each year when filing.
  • Standard deduction ₹75,000 and employer NPS (up to 14% of basic) are the main deductions left.
  • No tax up to ₹12 lakh taxable thanks to the rebate, with marginal relief just above it.
Not modelled here
  • Capital gains, crypto and lottery income taxed at special rates.
  • Professional tax, LTA, 80E, 80G, 80TTA and other smaller items.
  • Let-out property; home-loan interest here is for a self-occupied home.

How it's worked out
  • Employer PF and gratuity sit inside CTC but never reach your account each month.
  • Employee PF (12% on the same base) comes out of your gross pay.
  • Income tax uses the regime you pick, on gross salary including the variable part.
Monthly vs yearly
  • Monthly in-hand leaves out the variable pay and the tax on it.
  • Yearly in-hand assumes the full variable pay is paid out.
  • Real payslips differ as employers spread TDS based on declarations.
Old regime here
  • Counts only standard deduction ₹50,000, professional tax and your own PF under 80C.
  • HRA, 80D and home loan: use the regime tab for those.

RSU / ESPP value and tax

Your tax rate and the sale

Worked out

StepRSUESPP
At vest or purchase
  • RSU: the full market value on the vest date is salary (a perquisite).
  • ESPP: market value on the purchase date minus what you paid is salary.
  • Your employer withholds TDS on it and shows it in Form 16.
When you sell
  • Cost is the value already taxed at vest or purchase.
  • Held more than 24 months: long-term, 12.5% with no indexation.
  • 24 months or less: short-term, taxed at your slab rate.
  • No ₹1.25 lakh exemption; that's only for listed Indian equity.
Also keep in mind
  • Surcharge on long-term gains is capped at 15%.
  • Report the shares in Schedule FA of your return every year you hold them.
  • US tax withheld on dividends can be claimed as credit with Form 67.

Where sources disagree · read before relying on the RSU tab
  • Exchange rate for RSUs. Guides differ on whether the SBI TT buying rate is taken on the vest date or the last day of the previous month. Enter the rate your employer used (Form 16 or payslip).
  • Market value of foreign-listed shares. Some guides say closing price, some the average of open and close, some a merchant banker's value. Use the figure your employer reported.
  • Section numbers. The 2025 Act renumbered everything and published mappings disagree for HRA, home-loan interest and the standard deduction, so this page uses the familiar 1961 names.
  • PF wage ceiling. It rose from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month from 17 September 2026. Employers can still contribute on full basic.
  • Labour codes (in force 21 Nov 2025) count at least half of pay as "wages" for PF and gratuity, so a basic below 50% of CTC may give a higher PF than shown.

Glossary

CTCCost to company: everything the employer spends on you in a year, including its own PF and gratuity.
Standard deductionA flat amount taken off salary: ₹75,000 (new regime) or ₹50,000 (old).
Rebate (87A)Wipes out tax for taxable income up to ₹12 lakh (new) or ₹5 lakh (old).
Marginal reliefStops tax from rising by more than the income above a threshold (₹12 lakh, ₹50 lakh, ₹1 crore…).
SurchargeExtra 10% to 37% on the tax itself once income passes ₹50 lakh.
CessHealth and education cess: 4% on tax plus surcharge.
HRA exemptionLowest of HRA received, rent minus 10% of basic, and 50% or 40% of basic.
PerquisiteA non-cash benefit from the employer that is taxed as salary, such as RSUs.
LookbackESPP feature that applies the discount to the lower of the offer-start and purchase-date price.
SBI TT buying rateThe rate prescribed for turning foreign-currency values into rupees for tax.
