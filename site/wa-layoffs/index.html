How to read this

What a WARN notice is
  • An advance warning employers must file before a mass layoff or a site closure.
  • The federal rule covers employers with 100+ staff cutting 50+ jobs at one site, with 60 days' notice.
  • Smaller cuts don't show up, so this undercounts total job losses.
Reading the numbers
  • A notice is a plan, not a count. Final numbers can be lower if people are moved or rehired.
  • Months use the filing date, not the layoff date. Half of all layoffs here start 49–63 days after filing.
  • Company names are as filed. "Amazon" and "Amazon Fresh" count as two employers.
  • A few notices skew the totals. David's Bridal's 2023 notice counts 9,266 workers nationwide; Gebbers Farms files about 3,400 every November.
Where the data comes from
  • Washington Employment Security Department's public WARN database, copied as is.
  • Refreshed weekly by a scheduled job; the date at the top says when it last ran.
  • Five-year window: notices filed or starting in the last five years.

Glossary

LayoffJobs cut while the site stays open.
ClosureThe whole site or operation shuts.
Permanent / temporaryWhether the employer expects the jobs to come back. Nearly all notices here are permanent.
FiledWhen the state received the notice.
StartsThe first day of separations the employer gave. Some notices list a range; this is the first date.
WARNWorker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act, the 1988 US law behind these notices.
