Reading the number
  • Hourly rate = yearly pay ÷ working hours per year.
  • Meeting cost = each person's hourly rate × people × minutes ÷ 60.
  • Mixed currencies are totalled separately; the page doesn't convert between them.
Why 2,000 hours
  • 40 hours × 50 weeks is the usual round figure.
  • Change it to match your hours: 45 h × 48 weeks is 2,160.
  • Fewer hours means a higher hourly rate and a dearer meeting.
What it leaves out
  • Employer costs on top of salary: benefits, payroll taxes, office space.
  • Prep and switching time before and after the meeting.
  • The value of what got decided. Cost is only half the question.

Best meeting slots

How it works
  • Each place's local time comes from your browser's built-in time-zone rules, so daylight saving shifts are included.
  • Slots are checked every 15 minutes, so half-hour zones like India line up correctly.
  • Working hours are start to end; a 9 to 18 day ends at 18:00.
Daylight saving
  • Pick the actual meeting date: the US, UK and EU change clocks on different weekends.
  • A slot that works in October can be an hour off in November.
  • "+1" or "−1" in a cell means the next or previous calendar day there.
When nothing overlaps
  • The page lists the closest times, where most people are in hours.
  • Rotate who stays late rather than always the same place.
  • Night shifts work: set a start later than the end, like 22 to 6.

When does my notice period end?

Check your contract: it decides how notice is counted

How it's counted
  • Calendar days count every day, weekends included.
  • Working days skip your weekend and the holidays you list.
  • Months run to the same date N months later, less one day.
Last working day
  • If notice ends on a weekend or holiday, the last day you work is the working day before.
  • Leave offset moves that day earlier by the leave days you use.
  • Your relieving date on paper may still be the notice end date.
Check with HR
  • Whether day one is the resignation day or the day after.
  • Whether leave can offset notice; many employers pay it out instead.
  • Buy-out or waiver terms if you need to leave sooner.

About these tools

Privacy
  • Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.
  • Your inputs live in the page link, so a copied link shows the same result.
  • No cookies, no accounts, no tracking.
Where the time data comes from
  • Your browser's Intl API, which ships the IANA time-zone database.
  • No offsets are typed into this page, so daylight-saving changes come from that database.
  • A very old browser may have out-of-date rules for recently changed zones.
Limits
  • Estimates, not financial, legal or HR advice.
  • Meeting cost uses salary only, not total employment cost.
  • Notice rules come from your contract and local law, which this page doesn't know.
Method details · the exact arithmetic
  • Meeting cost: sum over roles of people × (yearly pay ÷ hours per year, or the hourly rate) × minutes ÷ 60. Yearly cost = that × meetings per year (default 52 weekly, 250 daily).
  • Time zones: the grid starts at midnight on the chosen date in the first place. Each 15-minute step is checked against every person's working hours in their own local time.
  • Notice period: start = resignation date (or the next day). Days: end = start + N − 1. Weeks: N × 7 days, or N × working days per week. Months: same date N months on, less one day; if that month is shorter, its last day.
  • Leave offset: counts back the given number of working days from the last working day.
